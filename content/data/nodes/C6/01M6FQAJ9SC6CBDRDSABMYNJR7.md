# Compensation

Historically, my knee-jerk reaction toward the design of TWOLD's setting has been to have islands of normal people in a sea of monsters.

However, in practice, I tend to focus on defining the islands and not the sea, resulting in a world which (following the analogy) is mostly land with moats of water between the bodies of land.

Part of the purpose of this document is to put more emphasis on fleshing out the sea and less on the islands.

# A world of villains

Previously this document was titled "A world of villains", but I shifted its focus to enemies.

Since the main characters are heroes, in this case there is some overlap between enemies and villains, but they aren't synonymous.

## Animosity

The idea of enemies instead of villains emphasizes animosity.

While animosity tends to exist between heroes and villains, the animosity is a side effect and there are more cases where they could have neutral relationships.

## Good enemies

The idea of villains means the other characters are essentially wicked.

Meanwhile, enemies can be either good or wicked characters.

There is a tendency for opponents of heroes to be villains, but it is also possible for good people to disagree or be forced to oppose each other due to difficult circumstances.

# Monsters vs villains

When conceptualizing the metaphorical sea around the islands, I tend to think of the sea has being populated exclusively by monsters.

Monsters are a useful ingredient for the setting, but they *are* primitive.
