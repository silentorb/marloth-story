I'm finding myself fascinated with the Act 1 of Path of Exile 2.

From beginning to end the story and setting of that first Act has resonated with me.

At a micro level, it's standard par for a dark ARPG and a variation of Act 1 from the first game, yet every detail feels a little richer, a little more creative, a little more engaging.

The experience is further enhanced by the most fun isometric ARPG gameplay I've ever seen, but that's outside the scope of this article.

# Notable highlights

Below I'll list some of my favorite touches in Act 1.

At times I'll contrast them with similar elements in POE 1.

## The Riverbank

The Riverbank is pretty well an update of the Twilight Strand from POE1, but it has some notable improvements to the setting.

1. More forest-like.  The beach side isn't just sand and rocks.
2. More edifices.  The Riverbank mixes [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXXP]].

## Clearfell Encampment

The first mini-town in Path of Exile 2, this is a variation of Lioneye's Watch from the first game.

Unlike Lioneye's Watch, which was a gathering place for exiles like the protagonist, the ClearFell Encampment is mainly a refuge for local refugees.

Also, unlike Lioneye's Watch, which originally had a military origin, the ClearFell Encampment is built and maintained by ordinary tradesmen and families.

In other words, the encampment has its roots in local community, not just soldiers and wandering adventurers.

The encampment is effectively [Post-apocalyptic](./01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592JM.md).

> 💡Technically, the first game was [Post-apocalyptic](./01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592JM.md) as well

## Clearfell

Instead of more beach settings like the first game, the following adventure location is more forested, but still with a mix of [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXXP]].

I like forests.

I also like how the title graphic for the forest areas in this game is a haunting twilit forest scene with menacing wolves.  It hints at danger.

## Grelwood

The first full blown forest in the game, and it is awesome.

I like how dark the lighting is.

This forest reminds me a little of Gloomwald from [[01M2DWY1GKAE3JWCP5EA2FEKX8]].  It's not quite as magical and exquisite as Gloomwald, but it's still sublime.

Early on this location has a tree full of hanged specters, and that is awesome.

I also like how with some elements such as a witch, this location has more of a [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5G]] feel, which is further emphasized in later areas.

## The Red Vale

A neighboring side forest with a red theme.  The red theme effectively blends autumnal beauty with hellish horror (streams of blood).

This is also the first area to start leveraging totems, and doing so in a very [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5G]] manner.

## The Grim Tangle

Isometric ARPGs, particularly POE1, usually have their share of underground tunnel areas.

This variant is a little cooler because it has an amber theme, with corrupted fungus and a crazed, diseased druid as the area boss.

While that is classic Diablo fare, it still feels fresh here.

## Cemetery of the Eternals

Popping out of the ground into an atmospheric cemetery is a pacing treat.

Contrast that with the first game, where the Act 1 cemeteries were:

1. Buffered by a relatively bland field area (instead of popping out of the ground)
2. Were arranged with their neighbors as more of intermediary areas instead of a distinct location
3. Were split into two cemetery areas, making each have a less distinct identity
4. Had less atmosphere
5. The POE2 cemetery ends in a memorable boss fight that bookends and pulls together the brief but satisfying cemetery arc

## The Tomb and the Mausoleum

In order to progress past the cemetery, the player must defeat two ghostly bosses, each one in its own underground area.

The stroke of genius which elevates these quests are the two bosses are husband and wife—two deceased nobles. ([[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXYH]])

The husband's combat is more brawny, while the wife's combat is more magical.

What further enhances the scenario is for each boss fight, the active boss periodically calls on his or her spouse for assistance.

The spouse will briefly drop in and double team the player, using attacks from the other boss fight.

It's interesting that even though they are villains in this context, every indication is that the couple loves each other and works together.

## Cemetery boss fight

The whole cemetery quests are granted by Lachlann, who is trying to reach his [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXYH]].

Defeating the two underground bosses enables the player to open the doors for Lachlann to be reunited with his family.

However, the doors lead to a section of the cemetery where all of Lachlann's family is buried.

In despair, Lachlann somehow turns into a powerful revenant that indiscriminately attacks the player.

It's nice that the initial quest giver for the arc to end up being the final boss for the arc.

However, unlike usual [Diablo](./01M2DXDWS6B4HJME7NAC76K315.md) style, it's refreshing that the plot twist isn't due to betrayal of the player, it is the final climax of a tragedy which the player must bring closure to.

## The Hunting Grounds

This is not as interesting of a forest as Grelwood, but it fits in well pacing-wise and has multiple interesting features scattered around its grounds, though not particularly profound.

## Freythorn

Freythorn is where the [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5G]] starts getting layered on thick, with almost a hint of [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY0P]] with some evil god and a cult of followers the player must stop.

Totems play an even more pivotal role here than in the Red Vale.

## Ogham Farmlands

At this stage in the game, having a farmland like this is a perfectly timed change to the setting.  It's a pleasure to arrive at the Farmlands every playthrough.

And this is such a cool farm area.

It's similar to the farm areas in [[01M2DWY1GKAE3JWCP5EA2FEKX8]], except those tended to drag on a little bit, and they felt more like a side corner of the world, where the Ogham Farmlands feels more nestled in the world.

Visually, the Ogham Farmlands fit the rest of the tone better than the Grim Dawn farmlands.

One of my favorite touches of the Ogham Farmlands is the way any fire in the fields will catch the crops on fire and spread.

It's more of a technical touch than a story or setting touch, but there is something about that detail which makes everything feel more alive and immersive.

## Ogham Village

To continue with the immaculate pacing, the Farmlands are followed by the village, and the village is in flames.

Really dramatic, hellish flames.

Once again, this isn't the first time I've seen something like this in an isometric ARPG, yet it grips me every time I set foot into Ogham Village.

It feels compelling, like big things are going down and its time for the player to step up and be even more heroic.

### The Executioner

But the most compelling aspect of Ogham Village is it's climax.

After fighting through the burning ruins of the village, the player arrives at a large courtyard with an executioner's platform at the far end.

Before the player can even see that, the player can hear a woman pleading for the life of her husband.

As the player approaches, a monstrous executioner beheads the woman's husband and she screams.

It is some of the best voice acting I've ever heard.  I might not be describing all of this if it wasn't her gripping performance which pulls the situation together.

Her scream is immediately followed by threats of revenge, which segues seamlessly to the boss dropping down to slay the player, who is about to become the avenger.

After slaying the Executioner, the player frees the woman.

The woman's name is Leitis.  After thanking the player, she sorrowfully escapes to Clearfell Encampment.

The player can directly return to Clearfell Encampment and further talk with Leitis.

The conversation is delicate, a balance of further mourning without being melodramatic, and leading to further revenge on the Count of the land and pointing to the next and final stages of Act 1.

> 💡The story flows so well in Act 1.

## The Manor Ramparts

I feel like this is a bit of a filler location, but it is still solid.

My favorite feature of this is background has lower levels with fantastic glowing red growth things.

Those things probably has some lore behind I haven't looked into yet.

I feel like I can see the design process now, where some art lead or game designer looked at the level and said, "This is too generic, we need something to spice it up."  And proceeded to add the perfect spice.  That one little flourish makes everything about the level feel fresh.
