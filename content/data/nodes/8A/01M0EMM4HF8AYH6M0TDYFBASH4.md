It is a common belief that superhero movies should only have one villain.

I find that boring.  ([[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY6C]]).

Spider-Man 3 is sometimes used as a cautionary tale as to why superhero movies should never juggle multiple villains, particularly three villains.

Yet [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5N]] elegantly weaves a narrative involving three villains.

However, it is derailed half-way through by Tim Burton's need to always have a misunderstood freak.

[[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5N]] could have been one of the greatest superhero movies ever if it wasn't for that derailing.

As it is, [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5N]] is a flawed masterpiece.

In particular, its organization of characters presents a valuable example of how precise organization can allow juggling of story elements normally seen as impractical to combine.

# The relationships

## Batman and Catwoman

Batman and Catwoman have a romantic relationship.

That romance is complicated by their being on opposite sides of Justice and Crime.

Beyond that, there is a borderline rivalry between them.

Out of the matrix of main characters, they are the only two masked vigilantes with secret identities.

That adds further empathy and tension to their relationship.

## Penguin and Catwoman

Penguin and Catwoman team up as supervillains.

Penguin brings out the worst of Catwoman.

Catwoman is a criminal, but Penguin is pure evil, and ends up trumping her in wickedness and ultimate backstabbing.

## Batman and Penguin

Batman and Penguin are the one straightforward hero vs. villain relationship.
