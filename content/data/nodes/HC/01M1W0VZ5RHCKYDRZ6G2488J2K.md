>

When trying to promote an ideology in writing, I've found three main means of doing so.

They form a continuum of contrast.

# Mind screw

The first method is the highest contrast: The mind screw.

Start with a protagonist that believes in an opposing worldview, then gradually bring them to the worldview held by the author.

I used in [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY6D]].

> 💡That approach was especially popular in the earlier 2000s when I started writing [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY6D]].
>
> It was an evolution of the mid 90s, where it was cool to have similar plot twists but without the character arc.
>
> In the early 2000s it became popular to have the plot twist where the protagonist realizes he had been wrong (Memento, The Machinist, Shutter Island, [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592FG]]).

# Antagonism

The second method is to have a protagonist that holds to the author's ideology, and must fight against an antagonist with an opposing ideology.

# Universal

The third method is to make that ideology an assumed default of the world.

The protagonist stands for that ideology, and no one opposes it.

# Method selection

I'm starting to suspect that the Universal method may actually be the strongest method of presenting an ideology.

Not every idea presented by an author needs an accompanying opposing viewpoint.

Doing so is not possible and an attempt to do so would unravel in infinitely granular metaness.

However, I think there is still a place for some contrast.

> Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds, for you know that the testing of your faith produces steadfastness
>
> James 1:1-3

At minimum, protagonists need antagonism, and it is optimal to align that opposition with the prominent issues of the story.
