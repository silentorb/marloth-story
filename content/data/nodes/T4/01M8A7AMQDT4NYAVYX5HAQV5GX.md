Over the past several years I've become fascinated by B-movies, particularly through the eyes of key YouTube channel content creators.

They are becoming one of my biggest sources of inspiration (next to WEBTOON).

# Era

First, I must qualify the scope of B-movies.

From what I've seen, the 80s were the pinnacle era of B-movies.

That peak bled into the 90s and gradually petered out in the digital age.

The decades prior to the 80s also produced many great B-movies, but that collection is spread out over many decades.

Any single decade has a difficult time competing with the 80s for B-movies.

# Genre

While any genre can be a B-movie, I am not interested in any genre.

I'm interested in the weird, fantastic, and spectacular.

Which goes hand-in-hand with B-movies.

While B-movies span all genres, they tend to gravitate toward the weird, fantastic, and spectacular.

Part of that economy and market.

A B-movie needs to grab attention and promise cheap thrills.

A B-movies can't shoot for being a slow, somber Academy Award candidate.

Also, B-movies tend to be more fringe and less mainstream.

They tend to attract maverick creators that don't want to play by corporate rules.

The B-movie scene is more underground.

Being niche also lends B-movies more toward nerd tastes.

> 💡 All of this harkens back to the classic pulp fiction and weird tales periodicals.

# So Bad It's Good

What interests me most about B-movies is how they do things normally unseen in mainstream movies.

I value quality, but quality, particularly when mass produced, requires some degree of convention.

B-movies tend to miss some of those conventions and result in crazy, outlandish features that capture my imagination.

I don't think those features are inherently bad quality, they are simply unachievable with current methods of achieving quality.

The challenge I want to invest in is figuring out how to take those diamond-in-the-rough ideas and use them in quality storytelling.

# Resourceful economy

> Creativity thrives under constraint

B-movie creators can appear shameless in light of the corners they routinely cut, but there is an art to making something out of nearly nothing.

When viewed from that angle, their MacGuyver-grade ingenuity is impressive.

What's more, while career B-movie creators tend to shovel schlock, there can be an astuteness to their schlock.

They have a nose for what people want.

And I'm not so much talking about base human interests, though that is mixed in.

I'm talking about people like Roger Corman, who had a cosmopolitan awareness of entertainment and trends.

Roger Corman peddled schlock, but he peddled a surprising diversity of it, and with consistent success.

While many his choices of content may have been base, he *did* have an artistic eye.

He knew how to engage audiences, it's just not so obvious because he was engaging audiences at minimal expense.

Godfrey Ho is another of my favorite examples.

Roger Corman turned reuse into an art form.

Godfrey Ho took reuse and remixing to the next level of art form.

# Humility

Unlike big budget industries such as Hollywood, B-movies tend to consist of minimally paid workers.

Out of the people who have worked in B-movies, I'm particularly interested in the people who made a career out of B-movies.

Their work wasn't glamorous, but many of them tended to still give it their all.

The B-movie side of the movie industry reminds me of the fork in the road I faced in college.

I chose television classes over film because the television classes looked more practical.

I had friends who were film students, and frankly most of what they did looked like a pretentious waste of time.

The television courses I went through taught technical and company skills.

Film school taught students how to press gang people to work on their movies.

Television classes taught students how to work as a team.

In that respect, studios that specialize in B-movies tend to be more of the "television" of film.

> 💡 However, I don't want to romanticize the humble B-movie worker.
>
> With lower standards, it seems like there are more frequently B-movie professionals who make a habit of working while inebriated and not caring about their lines.
>
> While big budget movies can face similar issues, they tend to place higher expectations on their workers.
