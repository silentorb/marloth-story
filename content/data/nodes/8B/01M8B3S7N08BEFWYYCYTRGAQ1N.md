# Overview

In general, I have never liked the idea of writing stories revolving around a very close community.

Some community is fine, but for some reason I don't like it when that is the entire scope.

Today (11/25/2026) it occurred to me that it would be useful for me to analyze *why*.

That is the goal of this document.

# Big picture

I think part of my aversion is I'm a big picture guy.

I'm hypersensitive to narrow-mindedness and naturally strive to avoid it.

While I might live day-to-day in a small world, I'm extremely aware that there is an infinitely larger world beyond what I see.

[[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY6D]] plays heavily on such concepts.

Because of that, my storytelling naturally gravitates toward very decentralized settings.

# Personal background

I'm a city boy.

While I've grown to appreciate small town country life, I likely will never relate to it.

I like being in a bustling city of strangers.

My stories likewise tend to reflect that preference.

# Social circles

This is a more specific case of the "Big picture" section.

A healthy life intersects multiple social circles.

The classic, extremely close narrative community model portrays life that revolves within a single social circle.

There may be secondary social circles, but they are grayscale compared to the main circle's living color.
