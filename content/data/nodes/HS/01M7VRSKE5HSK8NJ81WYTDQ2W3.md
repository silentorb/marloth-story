# Overview

Impetus normally means the driving force of the plot.

In Marloth narrative design, Impetus has a more specialized variant meaning.

# Tension and incentive

The standard meaning of impetus is very plot-oriented.

The concept I am trying to capture is related to that but my interest in it is independent of plot.

I'm interested in the emotional, driving tension as it affects the audience.

I'm interested in how such story elements can pull the audience in and build up energy where the audience wants something to happen.

It is tension and release, but just not any kind of tension and release, it's incentive to take action.

But while the resulting action is essential to the equation, my interest in all this is not the action, it is impetus as the force for incentivizing that action and savoring anticipation.

As I always like to say:

> Make the audience want something, then give it to them.

I don't want the audience to simply want something to happen, I want the audience to want the main character to make things happen.

This idea is about proactivity, not passiveness.

To further qualify this idea, this tension is not suspense, at least not in the normal narrative definition of the word "suspense".

This idea relies on a particular kind of tension, a tension of conflict.

It's the tension when an unstoppable force meets an immovable object.

It's the tension when two otherwise benign chemicals mix to cause an explosion.

# Bottled assertion

One of the most powerful cases of this type of impetus is bottled assertion.

In a way, that reflects my personality, someone who is introverted and cautious, yet internally passionate, and will wait and wait and suddenly take a stand and speak out and people are sometimes surprised by the mode change.

## Restrained strength

While bottled assertion can include cases where the protagonist is accepting circumstances until the circumstances become unbearable, that isn't my main interest in bottled assertion.

What interests me most is when the protagonist is restraining themself until the tension exceeds a threshold where the protagonist's moral/social code permits him to act.

It's the idea of restrained strength.

The restraint is admirable and builds sympathy, and further accentuates the final release.

A good example of this is Eliza Bennett in Pride and Prejudice.

She is either biting her tongue or giving people a piece of her mind, and each compliments the other.

# Plot

With all that said, I don't want to throw out any notion of impetus and plot.

Everything I described above still depends on the impetus *also* driving the plot.

It's a one-two punch.

The main point of this article is to define a variation of impetus that is *more* than just plot mechanics.

For me, impetus is something that aggressively pulls the audience into the story and forces them to relate to the protagonist as a proxy version of themselves within the story.
