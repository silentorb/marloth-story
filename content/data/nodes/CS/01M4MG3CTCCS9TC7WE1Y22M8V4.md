There are two ways to mix realism and surrealism.

1. Start with a realistic base and introduce surrealism
2. Start with an abstract base and make it more realistic

By default, I have usually tended to the former process.

However, there have been seasons, particularly when I was into [[01M4MG6W11X311E4WZZ2A83WS4]], where I leaned in this direction.

However, even with [[01M4MG6W11X311E4WZZ2A83WS4]], I don't think I had a clear sense of the surreal -> real process I'm now conceiving.

# Surreal to Real

The greatest blocker to my writing is various [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592DE]].

Yet the essence of surrealism is to defy such tensions.

If I just went full dreamlike, there would no longer be any tensions—I could make and break any rules.

But I don't want to go full dreamlike, I want [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXZ8]].

However, that doesn't mean the design process can't *start* with full dreamlike.

What if I mixed everything I want into a blender until it produced an unrealistic purée, then gradually solidified that purée as far as possible without losing the benefits of the blend?

# Execution

I suspect this would take some practice.

There are inhibitions that have been baked into my design process that I would need to let go of.

If nothing else, it should be a helpful exercise.

Quality design involves viewing a problem domain from various angles, and this would be a distinctly fresh perspective.

# Blending

At times I've considered what it would be like if I wrote a completely ordinary version of the story, and then Marloth-ified it?

What if I took that a step further with this surreal approach?

What if I designed or wrote two forms of the same story:

1. An ordinary story
2. A nonsensical fever dream story

Then I generated a median using vector math, where vectors A and B are multiplied to produce vector C.
