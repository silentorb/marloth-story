A political movement against magic.

# Plot motivation

The origin is a little fuzzy now, but if I remember right, the original motivation for this idea was ironically to better define the cultural stance on magic in [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592G6]].

Previously, I had envisioned the entire city being anti-magic.

Then I softened it and made the issue more controversial, where some were more open to magic while others were opposed.

An anti-magic campaign was introduced to crystalize the controversy.

## Nivana

Oh yeah, the even more core reason was [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY3Z]] is supposed to start out opposed to magic, and she can't simply be passively opposed to anything, she has to be socially fighting against it.

The whole idea of a campaign is very much a Nivana paradigm.

But it doesn't make sense for [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY3Z]] to be campaigning for what is already the status quo.

I didn't want to make the status quo full blown pro magic, so I settled for having it divided.

# De-emphasis on Nivana

In earlier versions of this idea, I had[[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY3Z]] as the most audience-visible proponent of this movement in TWOLD, but I don’t like that now.

The focus on Nivana was partially why I first cancelled this arc.

The idea in its original form felt pretentious.  It was unrealistic in bad ways.

I may still be able to achieve everything I wanted out of this arc without Nivana being the figurehead.

Maybe Nivana can still be a part of the movement without being unrealistically leading it.

> 💡 While Nivana shouldn’t be the figurehead for the campaign, she still can be the story’s *protagonist* for the campaign—the main representative of the campaign *for the audience.*

# Vilifying magic

The way these designs are going, it looks like a charismatic spin doctor is needed to assist Nivana in her campaign.

## Miles

Without creating a new character for the role, [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXZ7]] is the best off-the-shelf option.  Miles fits the role well because:

1. I’ve been looking for some reason for him and Nivana to interact with each other early in the story.
2. Not simply have I wanted Nivana and Miles to interact, but for him to also hurt her.  In this case, she won’t realize at the time that he has hurt her, though there may be a sense of abandonment as her world falls apart.
3. He is the king of lies, showmanship, and manipulation.
4. He is a magic user, and would have inside knowledge of magic societies and practices.  (He could be betraying them as a power move to reduce competition.  Also, his betrayal has some intrinsic emotional weight, though I may not be able to cast a lens on that area without damaging some of the mystery and suspense.)
5. This resurrects my original vision for the character, where Miles starts off looking like one of the good guys and later betrays them.  Here he looks like *sort* of a good guy.  Him later revealing his evil intentions need not be a surprise to the audience, but could be a surprise to some of the characters.

> 💡 Maybe a tragic event occurs which Miles promptly capitalizes on to vilify magic, and in a later part it is revealed that Miles caused the event.  Maybe in doing so he “kills two birds with one stone”, such as magically murdering someone who was about to reveal Miles’ own crimes.  (Though if Miles is planning on disappearing soon, revealing his crimes has less punch.  It might be better if the revelation somehow involves his larger plans and some of the more transcendent—TBD—mechanisms he is scheming to leverage.)

> 💡 When everything hits the fan and Nivana finds herself accused, there is no scene between her and Miles where Miles refuses to help her.  There is no scene period—Miles is simply gone.

## The problem with Miles

However, the problem with Miles is while he is charismatic and in one sense a leader, he is a shadow leader.

Miles is not one to stick his neck out, especially when the plan is to have things implode.

There may still be room for Miles to be involved, but similar to [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY3Z]], he also should not be the figurehead.

## Moving away from this again

As other aspects of the story have progressed, this plot idea is now antiquated.

Part of the problem is this story arc was framed around [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXZX]], and now that faction might not even appear in [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]] anymore.

With the direction the [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXYM]] arc is heading, the issue is less magic vs. science and more our city vs. their city.

The fear of [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592G6]] denizens should be less "evil influences are seeping into our city" and more "evil influences are coming from [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY1E]]".

The focus should be stomping out the other city (ultimately leading to [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592B6]]), not trying to build stronger walls.

Stronger walls and protection can still be a part of the story, but it shouldn't be a driving factor.

## Second thoughts

However, the story is more than [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXYM]], and magic is a central theme of the story.

I may have thrown the baby out with the bath water.

[[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXZX]] needs to go.  That can't be the focus of an anti-magic campaign.

But that doesn't mean there aren't still magic users for such a campaign to target.
