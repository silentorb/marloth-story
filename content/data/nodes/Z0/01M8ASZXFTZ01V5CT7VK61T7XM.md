> *Spider-Man 3* is sometimes used as a cautionary tale as to why superhero movies should never juggle multiple villains, particularly three villains.
>
> Yet [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5N]] elegantly weaves a narrative involving three villains.

There seems to be a fine line between a large cast being crowded vs. being elegant.

This applies to both macro and micro levels:

1. At the macro level, how many characters a story is juggling, either quantity, focal characters, or parallel plot threads, or wildly unrelated characters
2. At the micro level, how many (participating) characters are in a scene, and likewise how related are they

# Ensemble casts

Ensemble casts can work well, but they need the right framework.

For one thing, they generally don't work well in genre's that rely heavily on [[01M7VRSKE5HSK8NJ81WYTDQ2W3]].

Marloth is part of a heavily impetus-driven genre, so ensemble casts don't work great for it.

> 💡 One counter example is [[01M33AR1ZAGRT3NSQS3CQZS5W2]].

> However, one key ingredient is the bulk of the story revolves around a school which nearly all of the characters both attend and stay at the dorms, so the ensemble cast has a very close and constant domestic proximity, and that is something I'm not interested in for my own stories.
