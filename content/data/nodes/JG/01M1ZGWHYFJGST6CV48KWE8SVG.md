One of my primary goals for [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]] is [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY0Y]].

The way I've been trained to write, that means the book needs both:

1. [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T959283]] to be a major theme
2. [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T959283]] to be under attack and analyzed from multiple angles

But neither really fits with my motivation.

I just want an interesting story where the two main characters are a married couple, going through life and adventures together.

# Example - Sherlock Holmes

I could probably use almost any example as a counter-point but Sherlock Holmes was the first random case to come to mind.

Sherlock Holmes stories have many iconic features.

A random selection is:

1. He's a detective.
2. He lives in an apartment in Baker Street.
3. He has his friendly sidekick Watson.
4. He smokes a pipe.

In the books, there are little to ever cases where any of those features come into question.

Never does he face an existential crisis as to whether he should continue being a detective.

He is never forced into exile from Baker Street.

None of the stories employ a theme of sidekicks.

He never faces an underground criminal society of pipe-haters.

Similar to [[01M1W0VZ5RHCKYDRZ6G2488J2K]], the classic features of Sherlock Holmes simply are and are not questioned, challenged, or abstracted.

> 💡Some later live action adaptations did question, challenge, and abstract those features.

# Conclusion

At least for now [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T959283]] in [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]] will simply be a pleasant feature, not an [issue](./01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXW7.md) and not a theme that I a need to incorporate all over the story from various angles.

If while writing the story I come across cases where it would work well to add some tension to marriage or play with it as a theme, I'll do that, but I'm done feeling obligated to do so.
