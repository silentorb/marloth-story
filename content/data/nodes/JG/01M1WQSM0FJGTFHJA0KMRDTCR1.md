# The counterfeit bill analogy

An analogy is sometimes presented between doctrinal discernment and bank teller discernment.

The analogy states that when bank tellers are taught to detect counterfeit money, they are only shown real currency.

The idea is that if they become familiar enough with the real thing, they will intuitively notice when something is off.

The analogy associates that with doctrine, recommending focusing on learning truth instead of falsehood.

# Counter arguments

Two arguments are sometimes presented against that analogy and its application.

The first argument is that knowledge of a particular falsehood is needed to address that particular falsehood.

That is true to a point, but that argument talks past the analogy and is ultimately a fallacy.

That argument places fast and loose with tense and timing.

The entire context of the analogy is *preparation*.

How does one *prepare* for falsehoods one might meet?

It says nothing about how to address falsehood once one is met.

There is a difference between preemptively studying various falsehoods as a hobby, vs. responding to a falsehood that has arisen within circles you are responsible for.

For example, if a friend or family member starts claiming things that sound like heresy, there is reason to research those claims.

But even then, effective refutation requires far less research than is often suggested.

Too often, people fall back on study of falsehood because their own grasp of truth is weak.

If their understanding of truth had a firmer foundation, they could cut the falsehood off at the path.

# Infinite falsehood

> "Enter by the narrow gate. For the gate is wide and the way is easy that leads to destruction, and those who enter by it are many. For the gate is narrow and the way is hard that leads to life, and those who find it are few."
>
> Matthew 7:13-14

There is one path to life, and an infinite number of paths to destruction.

People invent new falsehoods every day.

If a person needs preexisting knowledge of each particular falsehood in order to counter each particular falsehood, then that person is doomed to failure.

In the same respect, if a bank teller can only discern counterfeits the teller is already aware of, that bank teller will be unable to discern new counterfeits.

# Secular media

Another argument sometimes raised against the counterfeit money analogy is that it impractically condemns consumption of secular media.

The argument is nonsense and almost not worth addressing, but I've seen it raised enough times to address it.

> 💡In this the arguments become a bit meta, where I am wrestling with how far to preemptively address arguments about preemptive preparation against arguments.

Most of my writing [[01KWN86X6PZXQP43T36924KCT8]] are secular.

All of them contain aspects I don't agree with.

When mining inspirations, I am not looking for falsehood—I'm looking for truth.

A fundamental part of my creative process is searching for dung encrusted jewels and washing them with [The Word](./01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY2W.md).

Short of [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY2W]], that is the case for everything created by man, just to varying degrees.

Some jewels are small, some are large.  Some have little dung, some are mostly dung.

# Contrast

When I was younger, I used to think I needed to debunk key falsehoods in my writing.

Since then, I've realized that is like chasing a mosquito through a swamp.

It is still good to debunk falsehood in my writing, but not for the sake of anything relating to that particular falsehood.

It is good to debunk falsehood as a foil for truth.

# Balance

With all that said, I don't want to throw out the baby with the bathwater.

Some falsehoods are more prevalent than others.

Some falsehoods are timeless.

While it is not essential to be aware of the classic falsehoods, it can be helpful.
