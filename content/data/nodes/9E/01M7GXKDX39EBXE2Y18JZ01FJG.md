# Overview

In [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]] I would like there to be secondary storylines where [[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592A4]] and [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXXW5]] are in a set of people which are metaphorically Christians.

# Background

[[01KWN86X6MFZQAJ1V36T9592GA]] is the Christ character of the stories.

It's not a great analogy.

The analogy is incidental and emergent.  It wasn't planned.

I guess the one quality it has is that I stuck with it—I wrestled with the topic for so long that anything to pass the criteria must have some value.

# Pale reflection

In the end, the only reason I reached any peace with that metaphor is with the mechanism that Tralvorkemen was a self-acknowledged reflection of someone better.

He is aware that he is a Christ character and not actually Jesus.

# Shifting metaphors

At one point during [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]]'s design I considered having a different analogy for Christ in each book.

However, it's so difficult to create respectful Jesus analogies and I struggled with just one, so I'm hesitant to burden myself with creating a variety of them.

# Orphans

As long as Tralvorkemen is the Christ character, the most immediately available analogy for Christians in [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]] is orphans.

James is an adopted orphan in [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]], and I've waffled on Adelle's situation, but at minimum her parents have always been in the background.

> 💡 The main issue is I just don't have enough creative energy to come up with two distinct sets of in-laws, and happened to lean toward James' parents because they are already defined.

> "Not all orphans are orphans."

I mean that as a play on Paul's reference of Israel:

> But it is not as though the word of God has failed. For not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel, and not all are children of Abraham because they are his offspring, but “Through Isaac shall your offspring be named.” This means that it is not the children of the flesh who are the children of God, but the children of the promise are counted as offspring. - Romans 9:6-8

However, the orphans quote could also have another applicable interpretation, that some orphans are adopted, effectively having parents.

Part of the idea of orphans in [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY83]] is a dreamlike idea of echoes of sentiment, like the characters are vaguely aware of a previous life where they knew each other and currently share a gravitational narrative pull toward each other.
