As mentioned in [[01M0EMM4HF8AYH6M0TDYFBASH4]]:

> *Spider-Man 3* is sometimes used as a cautionary tale as to why superhero movies should never juggle multiple villains, particularly three villains.
>
> Yet [[01KWN86X6NJZMP5ZESZTNDXY5N]] elegantly weaves a narrative involving three villains.

There seems to be a fine line between a large cast being crowded vs. being elegant.

This applies to both macro and micro levels:

1. At the macro level, how many characters a story is juggling, either quantity, focal characters, or parallel plot threads, or wildly unrelated characters
2. At the micro level, how many (participating) characters are in a scene

# Superhero agencies

It's not a coincidence that several of the inspirations above are extended superhero comic teams.

Those are specially designed for this kind of composition, both at a micro and macro level.

# Joss Whedon

Joss Whedon is the king of cramming lots of characters into a scene with rich, balanced chemistry.

His background is comic book writing, particular for superhero agencies, lends itself to that.

He also excels at macro character composition, though the inspirations I listed of his don't put much weight on that.

The first Avengers movie was a great demonstration of is skill at macro character composition, but corporate meddling ruined that composition in later MCU mashup movies.
